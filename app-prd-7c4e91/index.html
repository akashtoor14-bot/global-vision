01 The problem

Global Vision runs on four platforms that don't talk to each other

Members are asked to hold the whole community in their head across Telegram, GoHighLevel, a journal website and a marketing site. Three specific things break because of it.

The signal-to-execution gap

A desk posts a setup to Telegram. The member reads it on their phone, switches to MetaTrader 5, retypes the entry, stop and targets, and works out a lot size by hand. Minutes pass and the price moves. Plenty of members simply miss the trade — the signal was good and the outcome was nothing.

The signal-to-journal gap

Nothing connects what a desk posted to what the member actually did. The two records live in different systems, so Global Vision cannot answer the one question that would teach the most: the signal won — did you?

The platform tax

Telegram is not ours. No control over the experience, no data, no gate on who sees what, and our signals sit on a member's phone next to everything else in their life. Courses live in GoHighLevel, the journal is a separate website, and a new member is onboarded onto three logins before they have done anything.

02 Vision and principles

One hub. Every Global Vision product inside it. No external platforms.

These are decision rules, not slogans. When a design or engineering call is contested, it should be settled against this list.

  • Free forever. No paywall on any core surface. This is a standing constraint on the product, not an opening position.
  • The desks stay human. Automation and AI exist to support educators, never to replace them. Any feature that makes a desk less valuable is wrong by default.
  • Honesty over hype. A signal counts as a win when the market printed the take-profit — not when somebody marked it closed. This is the difference between us and the trackers members already distrust.
  • Education before execution. Every fast path — Tap to Trade, Vision AI — has to end by teaching something, not just doing something.
  • Mobile-first, Android-equal. The community is concentrated in the Philippines. Android is not a follow-up release.

04 Goals and metrics

Five goals, each with something we can actually count

#GoalHow we know
G1Replace TelegramShare of active members who opened a desk in-app in the last 7 days. Telegram groups retired within 90 days of general availability.
G2Close the execution gapMedian time from a signal being posted to the member's order being placed, for Tap to Trade users. Target under 60 seconds.
G3Close the journal gapShare of desk-followed trades that appear in the member's journal with the source signal attached.
G4Make journaling a habitWeekly active journalers, and the share of members holding a 7-day reflection streak.
G5One identityEvery app user authenticates through Supabase and reconciles to a GoHighLevel contact. No orphan accounts.
Deliberately not a metric

Revenue. The app is free forever and is not monetised. If a decision is being justified by what it earns, it is being justified wrongly.

05 Non-goals

What version one is not

  • A desktop or web version of the app. journal.globalv.me stays live and unchanged.
  • Monetisation, subscriptions, or in-app purchases of any kind.
  • Copy-trading between members. Tap to Trade copies a desk signal to your own account; it does not mirror another member.
  • Open public registration. Membership stays gated on GoHighLevel.
  • Brokers other than MetaTrader 5.
  • Replacing the marketing site or the client portal.

06 Information architecture

Five tabs, and two things that deliberately are not tabs

Tabs: Home · Signals · Charts · Journal · Academy.

Profile is not a tab. iOS caps a tab bar at five before it forces a “More” tab, and Charts earns a slot more than Profile does. Profile moves behind the avatar on Home, where it already had an entry point.

Vision AI is not a tab. It lives as a Vision Core button in the header of every screen, plus a card on Home. A sixth tab would dilute navigation for a feature that is a companion to everything rather than a destination. A floating bubble was considered and rejected: the bottom-right corner is already occupied across the app by send buttons, the last row of every list, and the log-trade action, and a floating bubble reads as a website support widget — the exact feel the app is moving away from.

Community chat is not a tab either. It sits inside the Signals tab, beneath the eight desks, mirroring how a chat list works in Telegram. Signals and conversation stay visually separate but one tap apart, and it costs no navigation slot.

07 Requirements

What has to be true for each surface

Priority is Must (version one does not ship without it), Should (ships unless something has to give), Later (deliberately deferred). Phase tags map to the release plan in section 09.

F1 — Identity and access

F1.1MustP0
Members sign in with the same Supabase magic-link and 8-digit code already used by the journal and the indicator portal. No new password, no separate app account.
F1.2MustP0
Account creation is gated on an existing GoHighLevel contact. Today the journal's /api/auth/exchange runs with shouldCreateUser: true, so any email that completes a magic link gets in. This must be closed before the app launches — see R6.
F1.3MustP0
One identity across app, journal and indicator portal. GoHighLevel stays master; Supabase is the shared front door.
F1.4MustP0
Sign-in works inside in-app browsers. The journal already hit a PKCE failure in that context; the app must not repeat it.
F1.5ShouldP1
Face ID or fingerprint unlock for returning sessions, so opening the app during a session is instant.
F1.6MustP0
Account deletion from inside the app, with a clear account-data export first. Apple requires this of apps that create accounts.

F2 — Signals

Eight desks, broadcast-only, plus the one room where the community talks.

F2.1MustP0
Eight signal desks, each a broadcast channel. Members read; they cannot post or reply.
F2.2MustP0
Desk list shows, per desk: last signal, relative time, unread count, and whether a trade is live right now.
F2.3MustP0
A signal carries instrument, direction, entry, stop, and a take-profit ladder of up to three levels, each with its own state — filled, hit, running, or missed.
F2.4MustP0
A level is only marked hit when the market actually printed it. No manually-graded wins. This is the honesty claim the whole product rests on and it is not negotiable.
F2.5MustP0
Per-desk follow toggle, which is what drives push notifications for that desk.
F2.6MustP0
In a desk, the message composer is replaced by a locked strip stating that only educators post there. The rule should be visible, not just enforced.
F2.7MustP0
Community Chat: one room, every member, text and images, with a pinned message.
F2.8MustP0
Moderation before launch. Report a message, mute a member, admin delete, and ban. Chat cannot ship without these — see R3.
F2.9ShouldP1
A broadcast-only Announcements channel, separate from chat.
F2.10MustP0
Educator posting flow on mobile: a structured composer for a new signal, and a way to update levels as they hit.
F2.11ShouldP1
Desk performance visible to members — win rate and results per desk, computed the same verified way as F2.4.
F2.12MustP1
All eight desks trade gold. XAUUSD is the only instrument Global Vision signals; the desk list should not imply otherwise.

F3 — Tap to Trade

The flagship. Blocked on a regulatory answer — see R1.

F3.1MustP1
Connect a MetaTrader 5 account, and support more than one.
F3.2MustP1
From a live signal, one confirmation sheet shows the desk's levels alongside the member's own execution: account, balance, risk percentage, lot size, dollar risk, dollar target and R multiple.
F3.3MustP1
Lot size is computed from the member's own balance and chosen risk, never copied from the desk. Two members following one signal should send different sizes appropriate to their accounts.
F3.4MustP1
Default risk percentage is a member setting and is editable on any individual trade.
F3.5MustP1
An "adjust manually" path is always available and never presented as the lesser option.
F3.6MustP1
The resulting position is written into the member's journal with the source signal attached — this is what makes G3 true.
F3.7ShouldP1
Open positions are visible in the app and closable from it.
F3.8MustP1
Plain risk disclosure on the confirmation sheet, in the member's line of sight, not buried in settings.
F3.9MustP1
A global kill switch so admins can disable Tap to Trade for everyone without shipping a release.

F4 — Journal

Everything currently on journal.globalv.me, rebuilt as native screens. No feature is dropped in translation.

F4.1MustP0
Dashboard with the six existing indicators: Balance, Trade win percentage, Peak day, Low day, Average risk-reward (displayed as a ratio, e.g. 1 : 2.4) and Total P&L.
F4.2MustP0
Calendar: month heatmap with per-day P&L and trade count, a weekly totals column, and month-level days, wins and profit.
F4.3MustP0
Daily: per-session cards showing trades, executions, wins, losses and fees, with Trades and Blotter views, and per-trade rating, tags and screenshot.
F4.4MustP0
Trade detail: entry, exit, stop, size, risk taken, maximum adverse and favourable excursion, P&L per second, rating, tags, screenshot, notes — and the linked signal where there is one.
F4.5MustP0
Trade Replay: candles with the member's entry and exit marked, play, pause and reset, timeframes of 1m, 5m, 30m and 1h, and speeds of real, 2×, 5× and 10×.
F4.6MustP0
Reflection: streak, pre-market prompt and answer, mindset check-in (mood, hours slept, stress 1–10), post-market prompt and answer, optional gratitude, and past entries.
F4.7MustP0
Analytics: daily P&L, performance by symbol, and account balance over time.
F4.8MustP0
Hall of Gains: monthly leaderboard by P&L percentage, minimum ten trades to qualify, opt-in only, with podium, ranked rows and the member's own rank pinned.
F4.9MustP0
Notes: Diary, Playbook and Screenshots.
F4.10MustP0
Accounts: several MT5 accounts, a starting balance per account, CSV and .xlsx import with deduplication, and import history.
F4.11MustP0
Journal settings: default starting balance, leaderboard opt-in and display name, and export to CSV or JSON. The member's data stays theirs.
F4.12MustP0
Import must work on iPhone from Files → On My iPhone → Downloads, which is where Safari puts a broker download. This is the most common point of failure today.
F4.13ShouldP2
Automatic MT5 sync, retiring manual CSV import. The likely path is the investor password rather than a broker partnership.

F5 — Academy

F5.1MustP1
Course list with modules, lesson counts, running time, progress and locked state.
F5.2MustP1
A continue-where-you-left-off entry point on both Home and Academy.
F5.3MustP1
Video playback that remembers position across sessions and devices.
F5.4MustP1
Lesson progress written back to the member's record so it survives a reinstall.
F5.5LaterP2
Download a lesson for offline viewing — valuable on mobile data, not required for launch.

F6 — Vision AI

A tutor scoped to Global Vision, reachable from every screen.

F6.1MustP1
Answers are scoped to Global Vision: how the platform works, troubleshooting, course content, and our own trading framework.
F6.2MustP1
Off-topic questions are declined, and the decline says what Vision AI does cover rather than leaving a dead end.
F6.3MustP1
Vision AI does not call the market or tell a member what to trade. It redirects to the framework and to the desks. This keeps Global Vision clear of giving financial advice.
F6.4ShouldP1
Context-aware: when asked, it can reference the member's own journal statistics — but only when asked.
F6.5ShouldP2
Chart screenshot reading: a member uploads a chart and gets a structured read — bias, confluence, the structure, imbalance and liquidity it found, and zone, invalidation and target levels drawn back onto their own chart.
F6.6MustP2
Every chart read is labelled a practice read, not a Global Vision signal. Required if F6.5 ships — see R2.
F6.7ShouldP2
"Compare with the desk": set the member's read beside what the desk actually posted, name the trade-off between them, and point at the lesson that teaches it.
F6.8ShouldP1
Answers link into the app — open a desk, a lesson, or a journal screen — rather than describing where to go.
F6.9MustP1
Reachable from the header of every screen. Never a tab.

F7 — Home

F7.1MustP0
Balance, today's P&L and a short performance trend, above the fold.
F7.2MustP0
The live signal from a followed desk, with a direct route into Tap to Trade.
F7.3MustP0
Win rate, average risk-reward and current journaling streak.
F7.4MustP0
A nudge when trades are closed but not yet journaled.
F7.5ShouldP1
Continue-learning card for the current Academy lesson.

F8 — Notifications

The thing Telegram currently does well. Parity here is a launch requirement.

F8.1MustP0
Push when a followed desk posts a new signal. Delivery speed is the whole point — this is measured by G2.
F8.2MustP1
Push when a target or stop is hit on a signal the member took.
F8.3ShouldP1
Push on a mention in community chat.
F8.4ShouldP1
A daily journaling reminder at a time the member chooses.
F8.5MustP0
Per-category controls, so a member can keep signals loud and everything else quiet.

F9 — Profile

F9.1MustP0
Membership status, member-since date, and the free-forever standing.
F9.2MustP1
Connected trading accounts and their state.
F9.3MustP1
Tap to Trade defaults, including default risk.
F9.4MustP0
Notification settings and desk subscriptions.
F9.5MustP0
Help and support, and sign out.

F11 — Vision Pulse signals

A ninth signal source that is a machine, and is labelled as one.

F11.1MustP2
Vision Pulse publishes its own gold signals, generated by the indicator rather than posted by an educator. It appears in the Signals tab under its own Automated heading, separate from the eight desks.
F11.2MustP2
Pulse signals carry the same structure and the same verification rule as desk signals — a target counts only when the market printed it (F2.3, F2.4).
F11.3MustP2
Pulse signals are visibly machine-generated. A member should never mistake one for an educator's call.
F11.4MustP2
Tap to Trade works from a Pulse signal exactly as it does from a desk signal.
F11.5MustP2
The Pulse feed is gated on the same $1,000 deposit as the indicators. Locked members see the feed exists and how many signals it posted today.

F12 — Trials

The only thing Global Vision sells, so the way in matters more than the price.

F12.1MustP2
A 7-day free trial is the primary way in for Vision Edge, Vision Pulse and the Pulse signal feed. Locked surfaces lead with the trial, not the price.
F12.2MustP2
The trial needs no card and no deposit, and ends by itself. Nothing to cancel, and the copy must say so.
F12.3MustP2
One trial per member, enforced server-side rather than by hiding the button.
F12.4MustP2
While a trial runs, every gated surface shows days remaining and a route to keep access permanently.
F12.5ShouldP2
When a trial ends the member returns to the locked state with their trial history visible — what fired during their seven days, and what it did.
F12.6MustP2
Trial state is per member and survives reinstall, since it is tied to the Supabase identity (F1.3).

F10 — Charts

A TradingView-grade chart inside the app, and the one place Global Vision sells something.

F10.1MustP2
Full-screen candlestick chart with real-time price, price axis, volume, and timeframes of 1m, 5m, 15m, 1h, 4h and 1D.
F10.2MustP2
Gold first. XAUUSD is the only pair the desks trade, so it is the default symbol and the only one shown as having active desks.
F10.3ShouldP2
Symbol switcher with a watchlist of other instruments, clearly marked charting-only.
F10.4MustP2
A live desk signal draws onto the chart as a position — entry, stop and take-profit levels with price tags, shaded risk below the entry and shaded target above it.
F10.5MustP2
Tap to Trade is reachable directly from the chart, not only from the desk feed. Same confirmation sheet, same member-sized risk (F3.2, F3.3).
F10.6MustP2
Vision Edge and Vision Pulse run inside the app. A member should never need a TradingView account or an invite-only request to use them.
F10.7MustP2
Both indicators are gated on a $1,000 minimum deposit in the member's trading account. Everything else in Global Vision stays free.
F10.8MustP2
Locked indicators are visible, not hidden. A member without access sees the indicator listed, marked locked, with the deposit requirement on the row.
F10.9MustP2
Tapping a locked indicator opens an explanation: what it does, the $1,000 requirement, and that access is lifetime once verified.
F10.10ShouldP2
The locked state offers a preview of the indicator running on the member's own chart, so the upsell shows the value rather than describing it.
F10.11MustP2
Deposit verification is manual today (a coach confirms it). The copy must not promise instant automatic unlocking.
F10.12ShouldP3
Standard indicators — moving averages, RSI, volume — free to everyone, toggled per chart.

08 Technical approach

Reuse what already works; build only what doesn't exist

Two of the four surfaces already have production systems behind them. The app should consume those rather than fork them.

LayerChoiceWhy
ClientReact Native + Expo One codebase for iPhone and Android. Android parity is a requirement because of where the community actually is, and a separate native Android build later would leave most members waiting.
AuthenticationSupabase Already the front door for the journal and the indicator portal. Magic link plus 8-digit code. GoHighLevel contacts stay master.
Journal dataexisting journal API Parse Server and MongoDB Atlas on Fly.io in Singapore, already live. The app calls the same API; the data model is not duplicated.
Signalsnew service Nothing exists yet. Needs a desk model, a signal model with per-level ladder state, and a worker that verifies a level against real price data before marking it hit.
ExecutionMetaApi (candidate) Already the intended path for journal broker sync. Sending orders is a larger commitment than reading history and needs its own evaluation.
PushExpo push Comes with the client choice and covers both platforms.
Vision AIClaude + retrieval Retrieval over course content, platform documentation and the framework, with scope and refusal rules enforced in the system prompt. Image input for chart reads.
Hosting regionSingapore / Tokyo The community is Philippines-heavy with no US members. Every region default should follow that, not the usual Virginia.

09 Release plan

Six phases, ordered by what hurts most today

P0
Foundations

Authentication with the GoHighLevel gate closed, the app shell and tab structure, Profile, and push infrastructure. Nothing member-visible ships in this phase, and skipping it is how the identity model gets broken later.

P1
Signals and community — the phase that kills Telegram

Eight desks, the educator posting flow, community chat with moderation, and signal notifications. This goes first because Telegram is the platform we do not own and the one members touch every day. Everything else can wait; this cannot.

P2
Journal

Full parity with journal.globalv.me, per section F4. Second rather than first because the journal already works on the web, so members are not blocked while we build it.

P3
Academy and Charts

Courses, progress and playback, gated on the hosting decision in Q3. Charts ships alongside it: the chart itself, the symbol switcher, and the two indicators behind the deposit gate. Signal-on-chart (F10.4) and chart-side Tap to Trade (F10.5) follow in phase four with the rest of execution.

P4
Tap to Trade

Account connection, the approve sheet, execution and write-back to the journal. Deliberately after the journal and after accounts are connected, and gated on R1.

P5
Vision AI

The scoped tutor first. Chart reads and desk comparison follow once the tutor is behaving and the framing in F6.6 has held up in practice.

10 Risks

Six things that could stop this, in the order they'd hurt

R1
Tap to Trade may be a regulated activity

Placing orders on a member's trading account is a different legal proposition from publishing a signal, and the answer varies across the Philippines, Canada and the UAE. This blocks phase four and needs a real answer from someone qualified — not an engineering judgement call. It does not block phases zero through three, which is part of why the order is what it is. Owner: founders.

R2
Vision AI could devalue the desks

An AI that issues free signals makes eight human desks look redundant, and the desks are the reason people are in the community. The mitigation is the practice-read framing in F6.6, which ends every chart read by pointing at what the desk actually did. If that framing is ever dropped, this risk comes straight back.

R3
Bringing chat in-house makes us responsible for it

On Telegram, content moderation is somebody else's problem. In our app it is ours — including the parts nobody enjoys. Named moderators and the tooling in F2.8 have to exist before phase one ships, not after the first incident.

R4
App store review is stricter for trading apps

Apps that touch trading and execution attract extra scrutiny: financial disclosures, clarity about what the app does and does not promise, and in-app account deletion (F1.6). Budget for more than one review round on the release that introduces Tap to Trade.

R5
The baseline device is not a new iPhone

A mid-range Android on mobile data is the device to design and test against. Charts, replay and image uploads are the features most likely to fall over there, and they are core to the product.

R6
The membership gate is currently open

Journal registration accepts any email that completes a magic link. Every product built on that identity inherits the hole, so it has to close before the app opens to the community — see F1.2.

11 Open questions

Answers needed before the phases they affect

  1. What is the current member count, and how many are active in a given week? The last confirmed figure is roughly 2,600 GoHighLevel contacts from May 2026, which is a mailing list rather than an activity measure.
  2. Who operates each of the eight desks, and will they post from the app? The educator flow in F2.10 depends on knowing who is actually posting and on what device.
  3. Academy: embed the existing GoHighLevel courses, or move the content into the app? This changes the size of phase three considerably.
  4. Which MetaTrader 5 brokers do members actually use? Import formats and execution both depend on the real spread of brokers, not the ones we assume.
  5. Is there a target launch date or a hard external deadline — an event, a campaign, a cohort intake?
  6. Who moderates community chat day to day, and what are the rules they enforce? R3 stays open until this has a name attached.
