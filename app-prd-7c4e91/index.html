01 The problem

Global Vision runs on four platforms that don't talk to each other

Members are asked to hold the whole community in their head across Telegram, GoHighLevel, a journal website and a marketing site. Three specific things break because of it.

The signal-to-execution gap

A desk posts a setup to Telegram. The member reads it on their phone, switches to MetaTrader 5, retypes the entry, stop and targets, and works out a lot size by hand. Minutes pass and the price moves. Plenty of members simply miss the trade — the signal was good and the outcome was nothing.

The signal-to-journal gap

Nothing connects what a desk posted to what the member actually did. The two records live in different systems, so Global Vision cannot answer the one question that would teach the most: the signal won — did you?

The platform tax

Telegram is not ours. No control over the experience, no data, no gate on who sees what, and our signals sit on a member's phone next to everything else in their life. Courses live in GoHighLevel, the journal is a separate website, and a new member is onboarded onto three logins before they have done anything.

02 Vision and principles

One hub. Every Global Vision product inside it. No external platforms.

These are decision rules, not slogans. When a design or engineering call is contested, it should be settled against this list.

  • Free forever. No paywall on any core surface. This is a standing constraint on the product, not an opening position.
  • The desks stay human. Automation and AI exist to support educators, never to replace them. Any feature that makes a desk less valuable is wrong by default.
  • Honesty over hype. A signal counts as a win when the market printed the take-profit — not when somebody marked it closed. This is the difference between us and the trackers members already distrust.
  • Education before execution. Every fast path — Tap to Trade, Vision AI — has to end by teaching something, not just doing something.
  • Mobile-first, Android-equal. The community is concentrated in the Philippines. Android is not a follow-up release.

05 Non-goals

What version one is not

  • A desktop or web version of the app. journal.globalv.me stays live and unchanged.
  • Monetisation, subscriptions, or in-app purchases of any kind.
  • Copy-trading between members. Tap to Trade copies a desk signal to your own account; it does not mirror another member.
  • Open public registration. Membership stays gated on GoHighLevel.
  • Brokers other than MetaTrader 5.
  • Replacing the marketing site or the client portal.

06 Information architecture

Three surfaces, one system behind them

The platform is a member app, a desktop site that does everything the app does, and a CRM that sits behind both. The CRM is not a back office bolted on afterwards — it is the thing that decides who gets into the app at all.

SurfaceWhoWhat it is for
Member app
iOS, then desktop web		 Verified clients Signals, Tap to Trade, the journal, Academy, community. Replaces Telegram outright.
CRM
corporate view		 Corporate Screening and verification, every client detail, conversation history, prospects, marketing, audit trail.
CRM
admin view		 Admins A restricted slice of the same CRM, plus the admin portal: attendance, leave, contracts, benefits, training, internal chat.
CRM
IB view		 Introducing brokers Their own clients only. Pipeline, activity, deposits, and participation in verification.

Nobody self-registers into the app. A prospect is screened in the CRM and only becomes able to create login credentials once verified. This inverts the usual order and has a consequence worth stating plainly: the CRM has to exist before the app can launch, because without it there is no way for anyone to get in.

The CRM duplicates what GoHighLevel does today — which the team is happy with — and adds what it cannot do: scoped access for admins and IBs, a full audit trail, and the admin portal. GoHighLevel is a closed product, so this is a rebuild of its behaviour rather than a fork of its code.

Inside the app

Tabs: Home · Signals · Charts · Community · Journal.

Community had to be a tab. Burying it in the drawer would work directly against the notification strategy in F8 — there is no point pulling someone back into the app if the thing you pulled them to is three taps deep. Academy moved to the drawer instead, with a card on Home: courses are binge-then-leave, community is daily.

The drawer (top-right, under the menu button) holds Academy, Products, Founders, Educators, Notifications, Settings, Help and Sign out — everything secondary, nothing daily.

Products sits in the header (top-right) on every tab root, permanently. Members currently ask admins what is for sale because they never find it; a static header icon fixes that without another floating element.

One floating button only: the support bubble, bottom-right, offering Vision AI or a human. Two floating elements would be the clutter this app is trying to avoid.

07 Design system

One reference, measured rather than eyeballed

Every colour in the app was sampled from a single reference image at pixel level. Nothing was matched by eye. The loop that produced the current build is: crop the reference, sample it, apply, render, measure the render, compare, repeat.

The reference is a published mobile-UI concept (Nexora Trading, by Design Kit Central). What we take from it is the visual language — the gradient system, card treatment, control geometry and type ramp — applied over Global Vision's own information architecture, content and brand mark. None of its copy, naming or screen structure is reproduced.

Why this reference

A trading app has to read as serious without reading as bleak. The reference solves that with one vertical sweep from near-white down to deep navy: the top of a screen is bright and open, the bottom is dark enough that figures and charts sit on it cleanly. Depth without darkness. That sweep, rather than any single colour, is the thing being copied.

Foundations

Two screen backgrounds. The light sweep is opt-in, used only where the body is entirely cards — Home, Journal, Community. Applying it everywhere was tried and reverted: on list and chat screens it put pale blue behind body text.

Type

Plus Jakarta Sans throughout. Every figure — prices, P&L, percentages, counts — sets in tabular-nums so digits do not shift width as values tick. On a screen where numbers update live, proportional figures read as jitter.

Accessibility

The target is WCAG 2.1 AA: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text. This is checked by an automated pass over all 49 artboards, currently 1,536 text elements.

The check has to be position-aware to be worth anything. Text over a gradient must be scored against the gradient at that element's position — a naive check scores white text against the pale end of a sweep it is nowhere near and reports hundreds of failures that do not exist. The light header band is also a pseudo-element, invisible to the DOM, so it has to be modelled explicitly. Without both, the pass reported 301 failures where 234 were real.

The last run: 50 known exceptions, each one deliberate.

08 Requirements

What has to be true for each surface

Priority is Must (version one does not ship without it), Should (ships unless something has to give), Later (deliberately deferred). Phase tags map to the release plan in section 10.

The letter says which surface a requirement belongs to. F is the member app and its desktop counterpart. C is the CRM. W is the admin portal. S is what an introducing broker sees. C, W and S are one system with role-scoped access, not three products — see C10.

F1 — Identity and access

F1.1MustP0
Members sign in with the same Supabase magic-link and 8-digit code already used by the journal and the indicator portal. No new password, no separate app account.
F1.2MustP0
Account creation is gated on verification in the CRM (C2.2, F19.1). Today the journal's /api/auth/exchange runs with shouldCreateUser: true, so any email that completes a magic link gets in. This must be closed before the app launches — see R6.
F1.3MustP0
One identity across app, journal and indicator portal. GoHighLevel stays master; Supabase is the shared front door.
F1.4MustP0
Sign-in works inside in-app browsers. The journal already hit a PKCE failure in that context; the app must not repeat it.
F1.5ShouldP1
Face ID or fingerprint unlock for returning sessions, so opening the app during a session is instant.
F1.6MustP0
Account deletion from inside the app, with a clear account-data export first. Apple requires this of apps that create accounts.

F2 — Signals

Eight desks, broadcast-only, plus the one room where the community talks.

F2.1MustP0
Eight signal desks, each a broadcast channel. Members read; they cannot post or reply.
F2.2MustP0
Desk list shows, per desk: last signal, relative time, unread count, and whether a trade is live right now.
F2.3MustP0
A signal carries instrument, direction, entry, stop, and a take-profit ladder of up to three levels, each with its own state — filled, hit, running, or missed.
F2.4MustP0
A level is only marked hit when the market actually printed it. No manually-graded wins. This is the honesty claim the whole product rests on and it is not negotiable.
F2.5MustP0
Per-desk follow toggle, which is what drives push notifications for that desk.
F2.6MustP0
In a desk, the message composer is replaced by a locked strip stating that only educators post there. The rule should be visible, not just enforced.
F2.7MustP0
Community Chat: one room, every member, text and images, with a pinned message.
F2.8MustP0
Moderation before launch. Report a message, mute a member, admin delete, and ban. Chat cannot ship without these — see R3.
F2.9ShouldP1
A broadcast-only Announcements channel, separate from chat.
F2.10MustP0
Educator posting flow on mobile: a structured composer for a new signal, and a way to update levels as they hit.
F2.11ShouldP1
Desk performance visible to members — win rate and results per desk, computed the same verified way as F2.4.
F2.12MustP1
All eight desks trade gold. XAUUSD is the only instrument Global Vision signals; the desk list should not imply otherwise.

F3 — Tap to Trade

The flagship. Blocked on a regulatory answer — see R1.

F3.1MustP1
Connect a MetaTrader 5 account, and support more than one.
F3.2MustP1
From a live signal, one confirmation sheet shows the desk's levels alongside the member's own execution: account, balance, risk percentage, lot size, dollar risk, dollar target and R multiple.
F3.3MustP1
Lot size is computed from the member's own balance and chosen risk, never copied from the desk. Two members following one signal should send different sizes appropriate to their accounts.
F3.4MustP1
Default risk percentage is a member setting and is editable on any individual trade.
F3.5MustP1
An "adjust manually" path is always available and never presented as the lesser option.
F3.6MustP1
The resulting position is written into the member's journal with the source signal attached — this is what makes G3 true.
F3.7ShouldP1
Open positions are visible in the app and closable from it.
F3.8MustP1
Plain risk disclosure on the confirmation sheet, in the member's line of sight, not buried in settings.
F3.9MustP1
A global kill switch so admins can disable Tap to Trade for everyone without shipping a release.

F4 — Journal

Only what reviews and improves trading: Calendar, Daily, Replay, Reflection, Analytics, Notes. Accounts moved to Settings, journal preferences moved to Settings, and Hall of Gains moved to Community.

F4.1MustP0
Dashboard with the six existing indicators: Balance, Trade win percentage, Peak day, Low day, Average risk-reward (displayed as a ratio, e.g. 1 : 2.4) and Total P&L.
F4.2MustP0
Calendar: month heatmap with per-day P&L and trade count, a weekly totals column, and month-level days, wins and profit.
F4.3MustP0
Daily: per-session cards showing trades, executions, wins, losses and fees, with Trades and Blotter views, and per-trade rating, tags and screenshot.
F4.4MustP0
Trade detail: entry, exit, stop, size, risk taken, maximum adverse and favourable excursion, P&L per second, rating, tags, screenshot, notes — and the linked signal where there is one.
F4.5MustP0
Trade Replay: candles with the member's entry and exit marked, play, pause and reset, timeframes of 1m, 5m, 30m and 1h, and speeds of real, 2×, 5× and 10×.
F4.6MustP0
Reflection: streak, pre-market prompt and answer, mindset check-in (mood, hours slept, stress 1–10), post-market prompt and answer, optional gratitude, and past entries.
F4.7MustP0
Analytics: daily P&L, performance by symbol, and account balance over time.
F4.8MustP0
Hall of Gains: monthly leaderboard by P&L percentage, minimum ten trades to qualify, opt-in only, with podium, ranked rows and the member's own rank pinned.
F4.9MustP0
Notes: Diary, Playbook and Screenshots.
F4.10MustP0
Accounts: several MT5 accounts, a starting balance per account, CSV and .xlsx import with deduplication, and import history.
F4.11MustP0
Journal settings: default starting balance, leaderboard opt-in and display name, and export to CSV or JSON. The member's data stays theirs.
F4.12MustP0
Import must work on iPhone from Files → On My iPhone → Downloads, which is where Safari puts a broker download. This is the most common point of failure today.
F4.13ShouldP2
Automatic MT5 sync, retiring manual CSV import. The likely path is the investor password rather than a broker partnership.

The thing Telegram currently does well. Parity here is a launch requirement.

F8.2MustP1
Push when a target or stop is hit on a signal the member took.
F8.3ShouldP1
Push on a mention in community chat.
F8.4ShouldP1

Per-category controls, so a member can keep signals loud and everything else quiet.

F8.8MustP1

F8.9MustP1

F10 — Charts

A TradingView-grade chart inside the app, and the one place Global Vision sells something.

F10.1MustP2

F10.5MustP2

F10.7MustP2

F10.8MustP2

F10.9MustP2

F10.10ShouldP2

F10.11MustP2

F10.12ShouldP3
Standard indicators — moving averages, RSI, volume — free to everyone, toggled per chart.
F10.13MustP2
A trade cannot be placed from the chart without the lot size visible. The order ticket shows size, risk percentage, stop, target, the dollar amount at risk and the R:R before the button — and the button itself states what it will do ("Buy 0.12 · risk $52.40").

F10.15ShouldP2

F10.16ShouldP3

F17 — Support bubble

F17.2MustP2
Vision AI hands off to a human the moment it cannot help, rather than dead-ending.
F17.3MustP2
The bubble must never sit on top of a control. It hides on scroll-down, returns on scroll-up, lifts above message composers, and content reserves space for it.
F17.4ShouldP2
VIP support shows a realistic response expectation rather than implying instant replies.

F18 — Design system and accessibility

The rules that keep 49 screens looking like one app. Full specification in section 07.

F18.1MustP0
Every colour comes from the token set in section 07. No screen introduces a one-off value; a colour that is needed twice becomes a token.
F18.2MustP0
The light screen sweep stays opt-in — Home, Journal and Community. Applying it globally puts pale blue behind body text on list and chat screens.
F18.3MustP0
The light header band is bound to the header element, never to the screen. Binding it to the screen makes it bleed onto body content wherever the header height varies.
F18.4MustP0
Text meets WCAG 2.1 AA — 4.5:1 body, 3:1 large — except the exceptions listed in section 07. Anything else failing is a regression.
F18.5MustP0
The contrast check runs over every screen and is position-aware: gradients are sampled at each element's own position, and the header band is modelled explicitly. A naive check reports hundreds of failures that do not exist and hides the ones that do.
F18.6MustP0
Touch targets are at least 44 x 44 points, including tab bar items and header buttons.
F18.7MustP1
All figures set in tabular-nums. Proportional digits shift width as live values tick and read as jitter.
F18.8ShouldP1
The app respects the system text-size setting. A trading figure the member cannot read is worse than one they have to scroll to.
F18.9ShouldP2
Profit and loss is never signalled by colour alone. Green and red carry a sign or an arrow as well, for the roughly one in twelve men with a colour vision deficiency.
F18.10ShouldP2
Motion respects prefers-reduced-motion; push, pop and sheet transitions become instant.

F19 — Registration, verification and access

Nobody self-registers. Screening happens in the CRM; verification is what unlocks the app.

F19.1MustP0
Downloading the app is not access. A prospect is screened and verified inside the CRM (C2) before they can create login credentials. There is no open sign-up path.
F19.2MustP0
Entry requires a broker account with a minimum $300 deposit, confirmed against the broker UID. This is an entry condition, not a maintained balance — losing money trading must never revoke access. The platform is free; the deposit is how the introducing-broker relationship funds it.
F19.3MustP0
The broker UID is mandatory and is the join between the app account and the CRM record. Every other client-side feature keys off it.
F19.4MustP0
Sponsor is mandatory and locks on registration. Only an admin can change it, and the change is written to the audit trail (C4) with the admin who made it.
F19.5MustP0
The sponsor field must capture the person who actually invited them. Free text alone has already failed — members enter things like "my wife". The field needs inline guidance, a name format, and ideally a lookup against existing members, with anything unmatched queued for an admin rather than accepted silently.
F19.6MustP0
Registration collects, at minimum: full name, email, phone, country, broker name, broker UID, MT5 account details, sponsor. All of it lands on the CRM record (C1) rather than in a form submission somewhere.
F19.7MustP0
Access is revoked after 30 days with no trade on any linked live account. Restoring it goes through an admin and is logged.
F19.8ShouldP1
The member is warned before revocation, not after. Open decision: the original notes describe a notification telling the member to place a trade. Enforcing an activity condition is ordinary; prompting somebody to trade in order to keep a benefit is a different thing and belongs in the R1 review. See Q8.
F19.9MustP1
Country is captured at registration and drives nothing silently — it is there so the platform can be reasoned about jurisdiction by jurisdiction.

F20 — Multiple trading accounts

Members hold a demo and several live accounts. Switching between them has to be trivial.

F20.1MustP0
A member can link more than one MT5 account: one or more live, and a demo.
F20.2MustP0
The active account is switchable wherever the account matters, and in particular from inside the Tap to Trade ticket — not buried in settings.
F20.3MustP0
Demo accounts are visibly marked everywhere they appear, including on the order ticket and in the journal. A member must never be able to mistake a demo fill for a live one.
F20.4MustP1
Members can run a product on demo before running it live. This is the stated reason they keep demo accounts, and it is the safest possible on-ramp to Tap to Trade.
F20.5MustP0
The 30-day activity rule (F19.7) counts activity across all linked live accounts. Demo activity does not keep an account alive.
F20.6ShouldP1
Journal entries record which account a trade was taken on, so demo and live performance never mix in the analytics.

F21 — Desktop web

The same product, reachable on a desktop browser.

F21.1MustP1
Every member-facing feature reachable on the phone is reachable on desktop. This is one product on two form factors, not a cut-down web version.
F21.2MustP1
One identity, one session model, shared with the app. A member signing in on desktop is the same account, subject to the same verification and the same 30-day rule.
F21.3MustP1
Build approach is an open decision. The app is React Native and Expo, so Expo Web can produce the desktop site from one codebase — far less work, with a ceiling on how desktop-native it feels. A separate web build is a second product with its own timeline. See Q9.
F21.4ShouldP2
Layouts that genuinely benefit from width — charts, the journal calendar, analytics — use it rather than rendering a stretched phone screen.

C1 — Client record

One record per client, holding everything anyone would otherwise have to ask for.

C1.1MustP0
Every client has a single record keyed on broker UID, carrying: name, email, phone, country, broker name, broker UID, MT5 account details (all linked accounts), sponsor, and registration date.
C1.2MustP0
The record shows current pipeline stage and how it got there — not just where the client is now.
C1.3MustP0
The record shows activity: last trade, trade frequency, days idle, and deposit total. These are the numbers that decide access, so they belong on the record rather than in a report.
C1.4MustP0
The record shows who last replied to this client and when, so no client sits unanswered because each admin assumed another had it.
C1.5MustP0
Sponsor is displayed on the record and is only editable by an admin, with the change logged (C4).
C1.6ShouldP1
The record links to the client’s journal activity and Academy progress, so a support conversation can start from what they have actually been doing.

C2 — Verification and pipelines

The screening path that decides who gets into the app.

C2.1MustP0
A configurable pipeline with named stages, from first contact through to verified. Clients move between stages, and every move is logged with who moved them (C4).
C2.2MustP0
Verification is the gate on app access. Reaching the verified stage is what lets a client create login credentials (F19.1). Nothing else grants access.
C2.3MustP0
Verification records the evidence it was based on — UID confirmed, deposit confirmed, sponsor confirmed — not just a status flag. A verified client should be answerable to the question "on what basis?"
C2.4MustP0
An IB can propose a client as verified, but corporate performs the final verification. The IB’s steps are visible to corporate before that call is made (S3).
C2.5MustP1
Access revocation (F19.7) is a pipeline state, not a deletion. A revoked client keeps their record and history.
C2.6ShouldP1
The pipeline supports rejection with a reason, so a declined prospect is distinguishable from one still in progress.

C3 — Re-verification

A standing queue rather than a periodic scramble.

C3.1MustP1
A dedicated section listing every client currently due for re-verification, with why they are in the queue and how long they have been there.
C3.2MustP1
Re-verification carries the same evidence requirement and the same audit trail as first verification (C2.3, C4).
C3.3ShouldP1
The queue is workable by more than one admin without collisions — claiming a client, or at minimum showing who is already on it.

C4 — Audit trail

The requirement that makes every other CRM requirement trustworthy.

C4.1MustP0
Every write is logged: who, what changed, from what to what, and when. Field edits, pipeline moves, verifications, sponsor changes, access revocations and restorations.
C4.2MustP0
Every admin has their own login. Shared accounts defeat the entire purpose of C4.1 and must not be possible.
C4.3MustP0
The trail is readable from the client record — the history of one client, in order — not only as a global log to be searched.
C4.4MustP0
The trail is append-only. Admins cannot edit or delete history, including their own.
C4.5ShouldP1
The trail is filterable by admin, so a suspected pattern of errors can be traced to a person and a period.

C5 — Conversations

Support history that survives the admin who handled it.

C5.1MustP0
Every conversation with a client or prospect is stored against their record, with the admin who handled it and timestamps.
C5.2MustP0
History is complete and permanent. Any admin picking up a client can see what was said before, by whom.
C5.3MustP1
Idle time per conversation is tracked and visible — how long since the client wrote, how long since anyone replied.
C5.4ShouldP1
Conversations can be assigned and reassigned, with the change logged.

C6 — Prospects

People who are interested but not on the platform. Today this audience lives in a Telegram channel.

C6.1MustP1
A contact route for people who are not clients — a way to reach an admin without an app account. This replaces Messenger, which carries these conversations today, so it inherits a migration of its own: existing threads have to arrive here or the history in C6.3 starts empty.
C6.2MustP1
First contact captures name, email and phone, and creates a record immediately. A prospect who is never captured cannot be followed up.
C6.3MustP1
Prospect records carry the same conversation history and assigned-admin model as clients (C5), so a returning prospect is recognised rather than restarted.
C6.4MustP1
Flagging, with a reason and the admin who set it: high risk, poacher, and whatever else the team needs. Flags are visible before an admin opens a conversation, not after.
C6.5MustP1
A prospect converts into a client record without losing history — the conversation from before they joined stays attached.
C6.6ShouldP2
Prospect source is captured where it is knowable, so the funnel can be reasoned about rather than guessed at.

C7 — Broadcast and marketing

The second Telegram replacement. The app replaces Telegram for members; this replaces it for everyone else.

C7.1MustP1
Email marketing to segments of clients and prospects, sent from the platform, with delivery and open tracking against the record.
C7.2MustP1
A broadcast channel reaching prospects who are not in the app — upcoming events, community news, the material currently pushed through the Telegram channel.
C7.3MustP1
Segmentation by the fields that matter: pipeline stage, country, sponsor, activity, flags.
C7.4MustP1
Unsubscribe handling that actually works, per channel, respected everywhere. This is a legal requirement in most of the jurisdictions the audience sits in, not a courtesy.
C7.5ShouldP2
Open question. Replacing a Telegram channel with email is a downgrade in reach and immediacy. Push notification to a lightweight prospect surface, or a web push, may hold the audience better. See Q10.

C8 — Integrations

One portal to manage the platforms, rather than one tab per platform.

C8.1ShouldP2
Meta API: ad performance and conversions visible inside the CRM, tied to prospect records where attribution allows.
C8.2ShouldP2
Gmail API: corporate email in and out, threaded against client and prospect records, including inbound job applications.
C8.5ShouldP2
Social media performance visible in the same place, so the business is measured from one screen rather than six tabs. Scope needs defining: paid performance is covered by C8.1, but organic reach, engagement and follower growth across channels is a different integration and a different job. See Q13.
C8.3MustP1
MT5 / broker data: deposits, trade activity and account status, which C1.3 and F19.7 both depend on. This is not optional — the access model does not function without it.
C8.4ShouldP2
Integrations fail visibly. A silent sync failure on C8.3 would revoke access from active traders, which is the worst outcome in this document.

C9 — Client activity tracking

The data behind the access rule.

C9.1MustP0
Trading activity per linked live account, refreshed often enough that the 30-day rule is accurate on the day it fires.
C9.2MustP0
A view of every client approaching the 30-day threshold, ordered by urgency, so admins can act before access is cut rather than field complaints after.
C9.3MustP0
Deposit totals per client, and confirmation of the $300 entry condition (F19.2).
C9.4ShouldP1
Trade frequency over time, so "active" can be understood as a trend rather than a single flag.

C10 — Roles and access scoping

One CRM, three audiences. This is access control, not three products.

C10.1MustP0
Role-based access with at least three tiers: corporate (everything), admin (a restricted slice), and IB (their own clients only).
C10.2MustP0
An IB can only ever see clients attributed to them. Scoping is enforced server-side, on every query, not by hiding elements in the interface.
C10.3MustP0
Roles are assignable and revocable by corporate, and every change is logged (C4).
C10.4MustP1
A sub-IB’s registered admins inherit a scope no wider than that sub-IB’s own (S4).

C11 — Migration off GoHighLevel

This is a full platform switch, not a new tool alongside the old one. Everything currently in GoHighLevel has to arrive intact.

C11.1MustP0
A complete export of everything GoHighLevel holds: contacts and all custom fields, pipelines and stage history, conversation history, tags, and the course and membership state. Anything not exported before switch-off is lost permanently.
C11.2MustP0
A documented field map from the GoHighLevel schema to the new one. Fields with no destination are decided on deliberately — carried, transformed, or dropped on the record — never dropped by omission.
C11.3MustP0
The migration is rehearsed against real data before it is run for real, and rehearsed more than once. The first pass is where you discover what does not map; discovering that during cut-over is the failure case.
C11.4MustP0
Migration is verifiable, not asserted. Record counts reconcile on both sides, and any record findable in GoHighLevel is findable in the new CRM. A reconciliation report is produced and kept.
C11.5MustP0
Conversation history migrates, not just contact records. C5.2 promises any admin can see what was said before. A migration that carries contacts but drops history breaks that promise on day one for every existing client.
C11.6MustP0
GoHighLevel remains readable for a defined period after cut-over, and is not cancelled on the day of the switch. Read-only access is the cheapest insurance available here.
C11.7MustP0
The Academy courses need a destination before switch-off. GoHighLevel hosts the course content and the membership state today, and nothing else currently does. This is the largest unresolved dependency in the migration.
C11.8MustP0
A written cut-over plan with a rollback: what happens if the new CRM has to be abandoned mid-switch, and who decides.
C11.9MustP1
Migration runs in cohorts rather than all at once where the data allows it, so a fault is discovered against a hundred records instead of every record.
C11.10ShouldP1
The audit trail (C4) records the migration itself, so a record that arrived by import is distinguishable from one created by an admin.

W1 — Time and attendance

The admin portal. W1 to W4 are HR rather than client data, which is why they sit apart from C — built in-house — see Q11.

W1.1MustP1
Clock in, clock out, and break states. An admin sets their own status; the timestamps are recorded, not self-reported after the fact.
W1.2MustP1
Named status tags — available, busy, on break, lunch, offline — visible to other admins, so a slow reply has an explanation before anyone chases it.
W1.3MustP1
Corporate view of attendance: hours worked, number and length of breaks, and how they compare across the team and over time.
W1.4ShouldP2
Status changes are logged and cannot be back-edited by the admin they belong to.
W1.5MustP1
Attendance and status records are employment records. They are retained for a defined period and are not silently editable, including by corporate.
W1.6ShouldP1
Staging lever. Since the portal is built rather than bought, depth is the only schedule control left. W1 and W2 — attendance and leave — are what admins touch daily and should ship complete. W4 can ship thinner at launch and deepen afterwards without anybody being blocked.

W2 — Scheduling and leave

W2.1MustP1
A team calendar carrying every admin’s schedule, so cover is visible rather than assumed.
W2.2MustP1
Leave requests — vacation and sick days — submitted in the portal, routed for approval, and logged with timestamps.
W2.3MustP1
Approved leave lands on the calendar automatically. Sick days are logged even when taken at short notice.
W2.4ShouldP2
Supporting evidence can be attached to a request where the policy requires it.

W3 — Contracts and pay

W3.1MustP1
Each admin’s contract is available to them in the portal, so contract questions do not have to go through a manager.
W3.2ShouldP2
Pay is adjusted for approved leave according to the terms in the admin’s own contract (W3.1). The contract is the source of the rule; the portal applies it and shows the working, so any deduction can be traced back to the clause it came from. Rules differ by jurisdiction, so the calculation is configurable rather than hard-coded.
W3.3ShouldP2
Leave balances are visible to the admin at all times — days taken, days remaining, by category.
W3.4MustP1
Contract and pay data is visible only to the admin it belongs to and to a named approver role. Not to other admins, not to corporate generally, and never through search. This is the tightest access scope in the platform.

W4 — Benefits and claims

W4.1MustP2
A benefits section covering what each admin is entitled to: dental, paid vacation, maternity leave, and anything else in their contract.
W4.2MustP2
Claims are submitted in the portal with receipts uploaded as proof, and routed for approval.
W4.3MustP2
Remaining entitlement is visible per category — vacation days left, dental coverage remaining — without asking anyone.
W4.4ShouldP2
Claim history and status are visible to the admin who filed them.
W4.5MustP1
Claim receipts are health data. Dental and medical documents are a special category in most privacy regimes and cannot be treated like client records. Encrypted at rest, visible only to the claimant and the approver, excluded from general search and from exports.
W4.6MustP1
Uploaded claim documents have a defined retention period and are deleted at the end of it. Keeping medical receipts indefinitely because deletion was never built is the most likely way this module causes harm.
W4.7ShouldP1
Staging lever. A launch-ready W4 can be a benefits list, a receipt upload and a manual approval. Automated entitlement calculation and claim history can follow. Nobody is blocked by doing the arithmetic by hand for a few months.

W5 — Performance

Measuring whether admins are doing the job they were hired for.

W5.1MustP1
Per-admin metrics: conversations handled, issues resolved, response rate, average response time, idle time.
W5.2MustP1
Corporate can compare admins over a chosen period, and see trend rather than a single snapshot.
W5.3ShouldP2
Metrics are visible to the admin themselves. People manage what they can see, and a metric used only for review is a worse tool than one used daily.
W5.4ShouldP2
Response-time measurement accounts for status (W1.2) so an admin is not penalised for a scheduled break.

W6 — Internal communication

Currently Discord: chat, voice and video.

W6.1MustP1
Admin-to-admin chat inside the portal, with history that corporate can review.
W6.2MustP1
A direct channel between each admin and the head manager for requests — days off, job matters — with timestamps and a permanent record, so requests cannot be disputed later.
W6.3MustP2
Voice and video calling. Discord is being switched off, so this is a replacement rather than an addition and it ships with the rest. This is the one part of the admin portal where "build our own" needs defining, because it is real-time media infrastructure rather than an application. Three readings, and they are very different jobs: writing the media layer from scratch, which is a specialist multi-year problem and not advisable; self-hosting an open-source stack such as LiveKit, which Global Vision owns and runs on its own infrastructure — almost certainly what is meant here, and the right answer; or a hosted provider, which is fastest but leaves this one function on somebody else’s platform. The middle option keeps the ownership principle intact without building media servers.
W6.4ShouldP2
Chat history is searchable and retained; it is part of the same record-keeping principle as C4.

W7 — Training and onboarding

W7.1MustP1
An onboarding path a new admin works through, with completion tracked.
W7.2MustP1
A permanent reference library: best practice, how to handle specific client situations, and troubleshooting for the broker portal. This is a reference, not a course — it is read mid-conversation, under time pressure.
W7.3ShouldP2
Content is searchable and versioned, so an admin can trust that what they are reading is current.

W8 — Vision AI for admins

W8.1ShouldP2
Vision AI answers admin questions about their own contract, leave process and benefits claims, so routine questions do not queue behind the head manager.
W8.2MustP2
Scoped to the asking admin. It must never surface another admin’s contract, pay or leave. This is the same scoping discipline as C10.2 and is not negotiable.
W8.3ShouldP2
It answers from the training library (W7.2) as well, so client-handling questions get the same treatment.

S1 — IB access

IBs run their own businesses on top of the platform. They need our systems, scoped to their clients.

S1.1MustP2
An IB sees their own clients and nothing else, enforced by C10.2.
S1.2MustP2
Per client, an IB sees activity, deposit total, and pipeline position — the same operational picture corporate has, narrowed to their book.
S1.3MustP2
An IB can see why a client is verified, and why a client who no longer qualifies still has access or has lost it. This is the transparency the notes ask for: a way to check our work as well as their own.

S2 — Verification participation

S2.1MustP2
An IB can move their clients through the pipeline and propose them as verified.
S2.2MustP2
Corporate sees the steps the IB took, then makes the final verification decision (C2.4). The IB proposes; corporate decides.
S2.3MustP2
Every IB action is written to the same audit trail as corporate actions (C4). There is one history per client, not one per audience.

S3 — A sub-IB’s own admins

S3.1MustP3
A sub-IB can register their own admins, who receive their own logins.
S3.2MustP3
Those admins inherit a scope no wider than the sub-IB who registered them (C10.4).
S3.3MustP3
Support from a sub-IB’s client routes to that sub-IB’s admins, not to the corporate queue.
S3.4ShouldP3
Corporate retains visibility of those conversations. Scoping controls who can act, not what corporate can audit.

Reuse what already works; build only what doesn't exist

Two of the four surfaces already have production systems behind them. The app should consume those rather than fork them.

10 Release plan

App build order, inside a platform sequence owned elsewhere

Platform sequencing lives in the MVP document, which orders the whole switch: client record and audit trail, broker data and verification, the access bridge, the app, the admin portal, migration rehearsal, then cut-over. That document is authoritative for what happens when.

The phases below are narrower. They describe the order the app gets built in, which sits inside the app milestone of that sequence. They were written when the app was the whole project and they still hold for it; they do not describe the platform.

P0
Foundations

Authentication with the GoHighLevel gate closed, the app shell and tab structure, Profile, and push infrastructure. Nothing member-visible ships in this phase, and skipping it is how the identity model gets broken later.

P1
Signals, Community and notifications — the phase that kills Telegram

Eight gold desks, the educator posting flow, community chat and feed with moderation, Hall of Gains, and the grouped notification system (F8.6–F8.9). This goes first because Telegram is the platform we do not own and the one members touch every day. Notifications belong in this phase rather than later — a community nobody hears about is the problem we are solving.

P2
Journal

Full parity with journal.globalv.me, per section F4. Second rather than first because the journal already works on the web, so members are not blocked while we build it.

P3
Academy, Charts, Products and the broker connection

Courses, progress and playback, gated on the hosting decision in Q3. Charts ships alongside: the chart, the symbol switcher, the free oscillators, and the two indicators behind the deposit gate with their trials. Products, Founders, Educators and the broker connection land here too. Signal-on-chart (F10.4), the order ticket (F10.13) and chart-side Tap to Trade (F10.5) follow in phase four with the rest of execution, since they all place real orders.

P4
Tap to Trade

Account connection, the approve sheet, execution and write-back to the journal. Deliberately after the journal and after accounts are connected, and gated on R1.

P5
Vision AI

The scoped tutor first. Chart reads and desk comparison follow once the tutor is behaving and the framing in F6.6 has held up in practice.

11 Risks

Six things that could stop this, in the order they'd hurt

R1
Tap to Trade may be a regulated activity

Placing orders on a member's trading account is a different legal proposition from publishing a signal, and the answer varies across the Philippines, Canada and the UAE. This blocks phase four and needs a real answer from someone qualified — not an engineering judgement call. It does not block phases zero through three, which is part of why the order is what it is. Owner: founders.

R2
Vision AI could devalue the desks

An AI that issues free signals makes eight human desks look redundant, and the desks are the reason people are in the community. The mitigation is the practice-read framing in F6.6, which ends every chart read by pointing at what the desk actually did. If that framing is ever dropped, this risk comes straight back.

R3
Bringing chat in-house makes us responsible for it

On Telegram, content moderation is somebody else's problem. In our app it is ours — including the parts nobody enjoys. Named moderators and the tooling in F2.8 have to exist before phase one ships, not after the first incident.

R4
App store review is stricter for trading apps

Apps that touch trading and execution attract extra scrutiny: financial disclosures, clarity about what the app does and does not promise, and in-app account deletion (F1.6). Budget for more than one review round on the release that introduces Tap to Trade.

R5
The baseline device is not a new iPhone

A mid-range Android on mobile data is the device to design and test against. Charts, replay and image uploads are the features most likely to fall over there, and they are core to the product.

R6
The membership gate is currently open

Journal registration accepts any email that completes a magic link. Every product built on that identity inherits the hole, so it has to close before the app opens to the community — see F1.2.

12 Open questions

Answers needed before the phases they affect

  1. What is the current member count, and how many are active in a given week? The last confirmed figure is roughly 2,600 GoHighLevel contacts from May 2026, which is a mailing list rather than an activity measure.
  2. Who operates each of the eight desks, and will they post from the app? The educator flow in F2.10 depends on knowing who is actually posting and on what device.
  3. Academy: embed the existing GoHighLevel courses, or move the content into the app? This changes the size of phase three considerably.
  4. Which MetaTrader 5 brokers do members actually use? Import formats and execution both depend on the real spread of brokers, not the ones we assume.
  5. Is there a target launch date or a hard external deadline — an event, a campaign, a cohort intake?
  6. Who moderates community chat day to day, and what are the rules they enforce? R3 stays open until this has a name attached.
  7. Home's hero card is copied faithfully from the reference, and the reference's own hero is low contrast — the account value measures 2.64:1 against a 3:1 target. Do we keep the exact replica, or darken the card behind the figure? This is the one place where matching the reference and readability genuinely conflict.
  8. Does the member get warned before access is revoked? Enforcing a 30-day activity condition is ordinary. A notification telling somebody to place a trade so they keep a benefit is a different act, and it belongs in the R1 review before it is built. See F19.8.
  9. Is the desktop site built from the app codebase or separately? Expo Web gives it from one codebase for a fraction of the effort, with a ceiling on how desktop-native it feels. A separate build is a second product with its own timeline. This is the largest single scope decision in the document. See F21.3.
  10. What actually replaces the Telegram prospect channel? Email is a real downgrade in reach and immediacy for an audience that currently gets instant broadcasts. Worth deciding deliberately rather than defaulting to a mailing list. See C7.5.
  11. Decided: the admin portal is built in-house, not bought. Recorded here because the reasoning matters later — the platform is meant to be one system that Global Vision owns end to end, and an embedded third-party HR tool would be the one part of the business running on somebody else’s product. The trade is schedule: the build sits on the launch critical path. The remaining lever is depth, not vendor — see W1.6 and W4.7.
  12. Where do the Academy courses live once GoHighLevel goes? GoHighLevel hosts the content and the membership state today, and nothing else does. This now blocks the migration rather than sitting beside it — see C11.7.
  13. What does "tracking our social media" cover? Paid performance is C8.1. Organic reach, engagement and follower growth across channels is a separate integration with separate work, and the two are routinely conflated. See C8.5.
