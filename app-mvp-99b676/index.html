01 The premise

The instinct to launch everything at once is right, and it is also the thing most likely to stop this shipping

The stated position is that nothing launches until the backend, the CRM and the app are all ready. That instinct is correct — a half-built platform in front of paying members is worse than no platform. But taken literally it describes four products shipping simultaneously, and that is not a plan anyone can execute.

This document takes the instinct seriously and asks a narrower question: what is the smallest complete thing? Not the smallest set of features, but the smallest arrangement where every member and every admin has a coherent experience with no dead ends. Something can be missing without being broken. A member who cannot find a feature is fine. A member who is stopped halfway through something is not.

The cuts below are made against one test: does its absence stop somebody, or merely inconvenience them?

One property of this launch removes the usual safety net. It is a switch, not an addition. Going live means turning off GoHighLevel and the Telegram desks at the same time — the old platform is not left running beside the new one. There is no version of this where members drift across at their own pace, and no quiet period to fix things while the old system carries the load. Everything has to work on the day, for everybody, or there is nowhere for them to be.

That raises the bar on two things in particular: the migration (C11), and the rollback plan in section 06.

A note on the word minimum. Four external platforms are being switched off together — GoHighLevel, Telegram, Discord and Messenger — and each one has to have a working replacement on the day it goes. That is not a minimum viable product in the usual sense of a thin first slice, and calling it one would be misleading. It is a full replacement of how the business runs, with the interior deliberately staged.

What this document can still do is separate the things that genuinely block the switch from the things that merely make it better, and be honest that the first list is long. The cuts in section 04 are correspondingly few.

02 The critical path

The CRM is the blocker, not the app

This is the single most important conclusion in the document, and it inverts the natural order of work.

The app is the visible product and the one furthest along — designed, prototyped, and largely specified. It is also the one that cannot launch on its own. Access is gated on verification inside the CRM, so on the day the app goes live, there must already be a working CRM with verified clients in it. Without that, nobody can log in. Not fewer people — nobody.

That reorders everything. The CRM is not the back office to be built once the app is done; it is the dependency the app is waiting on. Engineering attention should follow that, and the first milestone worth celebrating is not a screen — it is the first client verified end to end.

The audit trail is the one easily-cut item that must not be cut. Every other omission can be added later at normal cost. History cannot be backfilled — an edit made before logging exists is gone permanently, and the first thing anyone will want when something goes wrong is the record of what happened before it did.

Four blocks, and nothing that is not load-bearing

Enough CRM to get a person from first contact to a working app login, and to know what happened to them along the way.

Block 3 — App core

The reason a member opens it at all. This is the existing PRD, unchanged: the eight desks, Tap to Trade, the journal, community, Academy, Vision AI, and the F18 design and accessibility standard.

One judgement here. Tap to Trade should ship in the MVP, not after it. It is the flagship and the clearest reason to move off Telegram. Holding it back would launch a product whose main argument is missing — but it carries the R1 regulatory dependency, which is on the critical path in a way no engineering task is.

Block 4 — Migration

The block with no visible output and the highest cost of failure. Every existing client, conversation and course has to arrive in the new system before the old one is turned off.

Block 6 — Admin portal

Confirmed as launch-blocking. Discord is switched off at the same time as Telegram and GoHighLevel, so the team needs somewhere to work from on day one.

RequirementWhat it covers

W2.1–W2.3Team calendar, leave requests with approval, sick days logged.
W3.1–W3.3Contracts available to each admin, pay adjusted per contract terms, leave balances visible.
W4.1–W4.3Benefits, claims with receipts, remaining entitlement.
W5.1–W5.2Per-admin performance metrics and comparison over time.
W6.1–W6.3Admin chat, the manager channel, and voice and video — the Discord replacement.

W6.3 is worth costing early. Voice and video is the only item here that is real-time media rather than an application, so its effort is hard to guess from the requirement. Integrating an existing service is a normal way to deliver it. The point for this document is only that it ships with the rest, because Discord goes at the same time.

The admin portal is Global Vision’s own product, not a third-party tool the team gets sent to. How each capability inside it is delivered — written from scratch or built on something existing — is a build-time call and does not change what this document commits to. What does matter here is that the portal blocks launch, so its scope on the day needs deciding now rather than during the build.

Depth is the schedule lever, and it is a real one. W1 and W2 are what admins touch every day and should ship complete. W4 can launch as a benefits list, a receipt upload and a manual approval, with automated entitlement calculation following later — nobody is blocked by somebody doing that arithmetic by hand for a few months. See W1.6 and W4.7.

Running this data brings obligations regardless of how it is built. Dental and medical receipts are health data, not ordinary records: encrypted, visible only to the claimant and the approver, kept out of search and exports, and deleted on a schedule. Contract and pay data gets the tightest access scope in the platform — not visible to other admins, and not to corporate generally. W3.4, W4.5 and W4.6 cover this, and they are the requirements most likely to be skipped under time pressure and most expensive to retrofit.

04 What is out

Each of these is a real requirement, deliberately not in version one

Cutting is the point of this document. Everything below stays in the requirements document and stays committed — it is sequencing, not cancellation.

CutRefsWhy it is safe to cut
IB portalS1–S3 At current volume corporate can verify centrally. This becomes necessary when IB volume makes central verification the bottleneck — which is a good problem and a visible one.
Sub-IB adminsS3.1–S3.4 Depends on the IB portal existing. Second-order.
Desktop webF21 Members trade on phones, often on mobile data. Desktop is a real requirement and not an entry point. Also gated on an unmade decision (Q9).
Meta and GmailC8.1, C8.2 Convenience consolidation. Both tools work today in their own tabs.
Email marketingC7.1–C7.4 Prospect nurture matters, but it does not block a single member from using the platform. Telegram continues to serve this audience until it is ready.
Vision AI for adminsW8 The contract and benefits content it would answer from now ships in Block 6, so this becomes possible at launch — but a member of staff can still ask a manager. First thing to add once the portal is in use.

One cut deserves argument rather than assertion. Dropping the IB portal means IBs keep asking corporate for information about their own clients, which is the friction this platform exists to remove, and it asks the people bringing in business to wait. It is cut because central verification works at today’s volume, not because it is unimportant — and it should be the first thing built after launch.

05 Sequence

Ordered by dependency, not by visibility

The uncomfortable property of this order is that the first two stages produce nothing a member can see. That is the correct order anyway, and worth defending when it starts to feel slow.

M1
Client record and audit trail

The data model, individual admin logins, and logging from the first write. Everything else attaches to this. Building it second means losing history permanently.

M2
Broker data and verification

The MT5 and broker feed, deposit confirmation, activity tracking, the pipeline. Milestone: the first client verified end to end. This is the first moment the platform does something real.

M3
Access bridge

Verified record becomes an app login. Broker UID, locked sponsor, multiple MT5 accounts, the 30-day rule enforced in both directions. The CRM and the app become one system here.

M4
App core

Desks, Tap to Trade, journal, community, Academy, Vision AI. Largely specified already. R1 must be resolved before this ships, and it does not move faster because engineering is ready.

M5
Admin portal

Attendance, leave, contracts, benefits, KPIs, chat, and the Discord replacement. Runs in parallel with M3 and M4 rather than after them — it shares almost no code with the client path, so sequencing it late would add its full duration to the schedule for no reason. The lever here is depth rather than sequence: ship W1 and W2 complete, W4 thin.

M6
Migration rehearsal

Export everything from GoHighLevel, map it, load it into the new CRM against real data, and reconcile. Then do it again. This is a full stage, not a task inside launch week. The first rehearsal is where you find out what does not map, and finding that out during cut-over is the failure this whole document exists to avoid. The Academy destination (C11.7) has to be settled before this stage can finish.

M7
Cut-over and launch

Re-verify the existing community, run the real migration in cohorts, move members across, retire the Telegram desks. GoHighLevel stays readable, not cancelled. The riskiest stage: it is the one with real people in it, and the only one no amount of internal testing fully rehearses.

M8
After launch

IB portal first, then desktop web, then marketing and the remaining integrations.

06 What launch ready means

A checklist, so the decision is not a matter of opinion on the day

The stated position is that nothing launches until everything is ready. That only works if ready is written down in advance. These are pass or fail.

  • A new client can go from first contact to trading in the app without anyone touching a database by hand.
  • Every existing member has been re-verified, or has been told exactly what they need to do and by when.
  • The audit trail answers "who changed this and when" for every record, going back to the first write.
  • The 30-day rule has been tested in both directions: revoking an idle account, and restoring one.
  • The broker feed has run for a full month without a silent failure. A sync failure here revokes access from active traders — the worst outcome in either document.
  • R1 is closed, in writing, by someone qualified to close it.
  • Every admin has their own login and has been trained on the verification path.
  • Admins can clock in, request leave, see their contract and file a claim — the things they will do in week one.
  • Admin chat, voice and video work well enough that nobody reopens Discord out of habit.
  • Claim receipts are encrypted, out of search and exports, and have a working deletion schedule — not a planned one.
  • No admin can see another admin’s contract, pay or claims. Tested by trying it, not by reading the code.
  • Messenger conversations have arrived in the CRM, or the team knows which history did not survive.
  • Tap to Trade has been run on demo accounts by people who did not build it.
  • The migration has been run against real data and reconciled, at least twice, with the report kept.
  • Every client, conversation and course exists in the new CRM and can be found there.
  • GoHighLevel is still readable, and nobody has cancelled the account.
  • The rollback plan is written: if the app has to be pulled after launch, members know where to go.

The last one gets skipped most often and matters most. Telegram is being switched off as part of this. If the app fails in week one and the desks are gone, the community has nowhere to go — so the Telegram desks should stay dormant rather than deleted until the app has held for a defined period.

07 What could break this

Six things, in the order they would hurt

L1
The migration loses something nobody notices until later

GoHighLevel is switched off at launch, so anything not carried across is gone. The dangerous failures are the quiet ones — a custom field nobody mapped, conversation history that came across truncated, membership state that looked fine in a spot check. Mitigation: C11.3 and C11.4 — rehearse twice against real data, reconcile by count rather than by eye, and keep GoHighLevel readable afterwards so a late discovery is recoverable rather than fatal.

L2
The admin portal build expands and takes the launch date with it

W1–W4 is an HR system and it blocks launch, so its duration is the launch date rather than a parallel workstream that can slip quietly. HR features are also unusually prone to growth, because every person using them has a personal stake in one of them. Mitigation: fix the launch depth of each group in writing before M5 opens (W1.6, W4.7), and treat additions as post-launch by default rather than debating each on merit.

L3
Four platforms are switched off on one day

GoHighLevel, Telegram, Discord and Messenger all go together. Any one replacement being weak sends people straight back to the tool they know, and once a team is back on Discord it is hard to move them twice. Mitigation: keep every old platform dormant and readable rather than deleted, and treat a return to an old tool as a defect report rather than a discipline problem.

L4
Verification does not scale on launch day

Every existing member needs re-verifying at once. If that takes an admin ten minutes per client, the community size decides how long launch takes, and it is likely to be weeks. Mitigation: measure the per-client time during M2, before it is a launch-day discovery, and stage migration in cohorts.

L5
The broker feed is less reliable than assumed

Access, the entry condition and the 30-day rule all depend on C8.3. If it is slow, incomplete, or quietly stale, the platform revokes access from people who are trading. Mitigation: C8.4 — fail loudly, and never revoke on stale data. Build the fallback before the feature.

L6
R1 arrives late and blocks a finished product

Tap to Trade is the flagship and carries regulatory exposure. Legal review does not move faster because engineering is done. Mitigation: start R1 now, in parallel with M1, not when M4 is ready.
